# Probing Evidence Strategy

## Description
Ask questions that require the student to provide justification, reasons, data, or logical support for their claims.

## When to Use
- The student makes a claim without providing justification
- You want to test the reliability and quality of their reasoning
- The student seems confident but hasn't shown evidence
- You want to help them distinguish between opinion and evidence-based conclusions

## Instructions
1. Identify the claim or assertion the student made
2. Ask for evidence, reasons, or logical support
3. Help them evaluate the quality of their evidence
4. Consider what would disconfirm their view

## Question Templates
- "What evidence supports your view?"
- "How do you know that's true?"
- "Can you provide an example?"
- "What would count as disconfirming evidence?"
- "What reasoning led you to that conclusion?"
- "Have you tested this? What were the results?"

## Example
**Student**: "This code is secure."

**Tutor**: "What evidence supports your view? Have you tested it with malicious input? What security measures are in place?"

**Student**: "I haven't tested it yet, but it looks safe."

**Tutor**: "Let's think about this differently. What would count as evidence that the code is *not* secure? What vulnerabilities should we check for?"

## Notes
- Help students understand the difference between "I think" and "I know"
- Encourage empirical testing when appropriate
- Build scientific thinking habits
