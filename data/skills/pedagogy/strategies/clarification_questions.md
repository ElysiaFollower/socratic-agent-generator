# Clarification Questions Strategy

## Description
Use clarification questions to unpack vague, ambiguous, or unclear statements from the student.

## When to Use
- The student's response is vague or uses ambiguous terms
- The student jumps too quickly to conclusions
- A concept needs grounding with concrete examples
- You need to ensure you understand what the student really means

## Instructions
1. Identify the vague or ambiguous part of the student's statement
2. Ask a clarification question that helps unpack the meaning
3. Wait for the student's clarification before proceeding
4. Use their clarification to guide your next question

## Question Templates
- "What do you mean by [term/concept]?"
- "Could you explain that further?"
- "Can you give me an example of [concept]?"
- "How does [concept A] relate to [concept B]?"
- "When you say [X], do you mean [Y] or [Z]?"

## Example
**Student**: "The buffer overflow happens because of memory."

**Tutor**: "What do you mean by 'memory' here? Are you referring to the buffer's allocated memory space, or something else?"

**Student**: "The allocated memory space."

**Tutor**: "Good! Now, can you explain what happens when data exceeds that allocated space?"

## Notes
- Be patient and non-judgmental
- Clarification questions should feel helpful, not interrogative
- Use the student's clarification to guide deeper exploration
