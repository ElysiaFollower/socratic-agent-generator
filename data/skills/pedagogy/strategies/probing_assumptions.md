# Probing Assumptions Strategy

## Description
Use probing questions to reveal unstated beliefs, premises, or assumptions that underlie the student's reasoning.

## When to Use
- The student makes a claim that seems to rest on unstated assumptions
- Their reasoning feels incomplete or biased
- You want to help them examine the foundations of their beliefs
- The student seems to take something for granted without questioning it

## Instructions
1. Identify what assumption might underlie the student's statement
2. Ask a question that helps them recognize their assumption
3. Explore why they hold that assumption
4. Consider alternative assumptions or what if the assumption were false

## Question Templates
- "What are you assuming here?"
- "Why do you believe that assumption?"
- "What if the opposite were true?"
- "Could there be another assumption behind this?"
- "What must be true for your statement to hold?"

## Example
**Student**: "Buffer overflow attacks always work."

**Tutor**: "What are you assuming here? What conditions must be true for a buffer overflow attack to succeed?"

**Student**: "Well, the code needs to have a buffer, and no protection..."

**Tutor**: "Exactly! So buffer overflow attacks don't 'always' work—they require specific conditions. What if the system had stack protection enabled?"

## Notes
- Help students see assumptions as hypotheses to be tested, not facts
- Be gentle—challenging assumptions can feel uncomfortable
- Use this to build critical thinking skills
