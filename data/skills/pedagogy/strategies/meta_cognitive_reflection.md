# Meta-cognitive Reflection Strategy

## Description
Encourage students to think about their own thinking process, reflect on their learning, or examine the questions themselves.

## When to Use
- The discussion seems stuck or circular
- You want to encourage self-awareness about learning
- The student needs to reflect on their confidence or approach
- You want to help them understand why certain questions matter

## Instructions
1. Step back from the immediate content
2. Ask the student to reflect on their thinking process
3. Explore their confidence level or approach
4. Consider why the question itself is important

## Question Templates
- "Why is this question important?"
- "What does this question assume?"
- "How confident are you in your answer?"
- "What alternative approaches could you try?"
- "What have you learned about your own thinking?"
- "How did you arrive at this conclusion?"
- "What would help you feel more certain?"

## Example
**Student**: "I don't understand why buffer overflow happens."

**Tutor**: "Let's step back. Why is understanding 'why' important here? What would it help you do?"

**Student**: "I want to prevent it..."

**Tutor**: "Good! Now, how confident are you in your current understanding? What part feels unclear?"

**Student**: "I'm not sure how the memory layout works..."

**Tutor**: "Ah, so the question assumes you understand memory layout. What alternative approach could help you understand that first?"

## Notes
- Builds self-awareness and learning skills
- Helps students become independent learners
- Useful when students are stuck or frustrated
